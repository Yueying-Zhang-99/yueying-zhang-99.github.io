RESEARCH QUESTION · 研究问题

How can visitors experience history where it happened?

At Quanzhou Confucian Temple, the buildings that survive today tell only part of the story. Earlier layouts, teaching practices and rituals are harder to grasp through signs and static displays. This thesis asks how an on-site mixed-reality game could make those historical layers understandable while keeping the physical site at the centre of the visit.

研究以泉州府文庙为例，探讨如何让访客在真实遗产空间中理解不同时期的格局、礼制与文教活动，以及这些变化背后的复合遗产价值。

DESIGN METHOD · 设计方法

Turning historical evidence into a spacetime puzzle

  1. Read the site. Review comparable heritage games, trace the temple’s historical development, and examine the relationship between the surviving buildings and how visitors experience them today.
  2. Write the route. Connect episodes such as changes in site selection, rituals in the central complex, the layout of commemorative shrines, and the disruption and repair of ritual spaces to places visitors can explore.
  3. Make each episode playable. In 「时空巡礼·万象遗章」, players follow clues, manipulate reconstructed elements and encounter historical characters and ritual scenes. Completing tasks earns puzzle pieces that help connect separate episodes into a longer history.

「时空拼图」把史料中的事件、建筑构件与场所活动转译成关卡、场景和操作。访客通过收集拼片，把各个地点发生的故事重新拼成文庙的历史脉络。

PROTOTYPE · 原型开发

Aligning the virtual scenes with the real temple

After testing mobile AR, the project developed a headset prototype in Unity for Meta Quest 3S. Instead of relying on a single anchor for the whole complex, the prototype divided the site into zones and used spatial anchors to align virtual content within each area. This avoided installing equipment on the historic fabric. A skip option let visitors continue when tracking drift made an interaction unreachable.

原型采用 Unity 与 Meta Quest 3S，以分区扫描和空间锚点完成虚实配准；在不接触建筑本体的前提下，将虚拟复原场景与现场对应。现场光线、遮挡与高度偏移仍会影响定位稳定性。

FIELD STUDY · 实地测试

What changed in the visit?

The MR group included 20 participants: ten local residents and ten visitors. The study combined experience ratings, movement traces and a history quiz. A comparison group answered the same quiz after a conventional visit, allowing the thesis to compare how the two visits supported factual recall, interpretation and evaluative judgement.

One result: For the seven interpretive questions, visitors in the MR group answered 68.6% correctly, compared with 27.1% in the conventional-visit group. The corresponding rates for local residents were 81.4% and 64.3% (thesis Tables 5-4 and 5-5).

The movement traces helped show where participants stopped and explored. The quiz results suggest that the prototype supported historical understanding at this site, particularly for visitors with less prior familiarity. The study’s small sample, single site and short-term assessment limit what can be inferred about other settings or long-term learning.

研究结合体验量表、时空轨迹与历史问答，比较常规参观与 MR 体验。结果显示，MR 组在历史知识和建筑文化解读方面表现更好，外地游客与本地市民的差距有所缩小；结论仍需在更多场地和更大样本中检验。

CONTRIBUTION · 研究贡献

A workflow for designing on-site heritage experiences

The thesis connects historical analysis, spatial storytelling, prototype development and field evaluation. Its design approach starts with what a particular place can tell visitors, then chooses the game tasks and digital reconstructions that let them discover that story on site.

论文总结了一条从遗产价值分析、游戏化转译到混合现实开发与实地验证的方法，为其他建成遗产地的在地数字阐释提供参考。
