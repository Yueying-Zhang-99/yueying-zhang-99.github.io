An Embodied Interaction Framework for Analytical Engagement in Virtual Architectural Exhibitions

Zhang Y., Lo T. T.*

Abstract. Online exhibitions, particularly graduation shows, have become a vital medium for architectural communication by extending engagement beyond physical constraints. Although embodied spatial cognition suggests that interaction can support situated interpretation, many implementations still function as static galleries that rely on audiences already knowing how to read plans, infer scale, and grasp project context. This study addresses that gap by proposing an Embodied Interaction Framework, developed and examined in an online graduation show set within a virtual model of a campus building, to shift visitors from passive viewers to active agents of analysis and critique. The framework comprises three levels, with spatial controls enabling perspective shifts across scales, object-level tools supporting inspection and comparison, and a contextual layer coupling exhibits with spatiotemporal architectural settings to guide interpretation. Based on qualitative feedback following a session of free exploration, this framework enables analytical engagement by first anchoring users in a familiar spatial presence and then providing interaction that transcends physical constraints to deepen comprehension beyond static display. This study offers a transferable model applying to virtual exhibition design and the broader practice of embodied space design.
