Digital Heritage Engagement through XR, Gamification, and Human–AI Collaboration

This page outlines an evolving doctoral research direction. The final scope, title, and methodology will be refined through doctoral study.

Research Direction

The proposed research explores how extended reality, playful interaction, and human–AI collaboration can support public interpretation and meaningful engagement with built heritage. It provisionally focuses on participatory experience design, embodied interaction, and new forms of digital heritage communication.

Provisional Questions

  • How can XR move heritage audiences from passive viewing toward active interpretation?
  • How can gamification support sustained, meaningful engagement without trivialising heritage values?
  • How might human–AI collaboration enable plural and participatory heritage narratives?

Generative Video as a Participatory Medium: Enhancing Public Cognition of Built Heritage through Human-AI Collaborative Interpretation (HACI) Workflow

Zhang Y., Chen X., Lo T.*, Wang Z.

Built HeritageGenerative VideoHuman–AI

Traditional interpretation approaches for built heritage often position the public as passive viewers and limit their ability to actively engage with the intangible social and cultural values in heritage spaces. Although current generative AI (GenAI) can create dynamic and personalised content, its application in digital heritage still focuses on automated production rather than supporting the public’s cognitive engagement with heritage values. Therefore, this study develops and evaluates a Human-AI Collaborative Interpretation (HACI) workflow that uses spatially constrained image-to-video generation based on the Veo3 model to interact with heritage-related Chinese classical paintings, enabling unprofessional visitors to animate activities situated in depicted spaces. A between-subjects experiment with 41 participants was conducted to compare the HACI workflow with traditional interpretation approaches. According to the analysis of questionnaire responses and 76 generation logs, the HACI workflow successfully improved users’ deeper understanding of the relationship between tangible heritage spaces and intangible cultural activities, although it remains necessary to improve the alignment between prompt content and image complexity for more consistent generated outcomes. These findings suggest that generative video, when used as a participatory medium, can shift audiences from passive viewing towards active engagement with historical spaces depicted in paintings.

Keywords. Built Heritage; Value Interpretation and Presentation; Generative Video; Human-AI Collaboration; Public Cognition.
