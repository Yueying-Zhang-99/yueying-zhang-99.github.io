Generative Video as a Participatory Medium: Enhancing Public Cognition of Built Heritage through Human-AI Collaborative Interpretation (HACI) Workflow

Zhang Y., Chen X., Lo T.*, Wang Z.

Built HeritageGenerative VideoHuman–AI
Generative Video Example

Abstract

Traditional interpretation approaches for built heritage often position the public as passive viewers and limit their ability to actively engage with the intangible social and cultural values in heritage spaces. Although current generative AI (GenAI) can create dynamic and personalised content, its application in digital heritage still focuses on automated production rather than supporting the public’s cognitive engagement with heritage values. Therefore, this study develops and evaluates a Human-AI Collaborative Interpretation (HACI) workflow that uses spatially constrained image-to-video generation based on the Veo3 model to interact with heritage-related Chinese classical paintings, enabling unprofessional visitors to animate activities situated in depicted spaces. A between-subjects experiment with 41 participants was conducted to compare the HACI workflow with traditional interpretation approaches. According to the analysis of questionnaire responses and 76 generation logs, the HACI workflow successfully improved users’ deeper understanding of the relationship between tangible heritage spaces and intangible cultural activities, although it remains necessary to improve the alignment between prompt content and image complexity for more consistent generated outcomes. These findings suggest that generative video, when used as a participatory medium, can shift audiences from passive viewing towards active engagement with historical spaces depicted in paintings.

Keywords. Built Heritage; Value Interpretation and Presentation; Generative Video; Human-AI Collaboration; Public Cognition.

44th Conference on Education and Research in Computer Aided Architectural Design in Europe, eCAADe 2026, Vol. 3, pp. 377–386, 7–11 September 2026.

An Embodied Interaction Framework for Analytical Engagement in Virtual Architectural Exhibitions

Zhang Y., Lo T. T.*

VRVirtual ExhibitionEmbodied Interaction
Embodied Interaction Framework

Abstract

Online exhibitions, particularly graduation shows, have become a vital medium for architectural communication by extending engagement beyond physical constraints. Although embodied spatial cognition suggests that interaction can support situated interpretation, many implementations still function as static galleries that rely on audiences already knowing how to read plans, infer scale, and grasp project context. This study addresses that gap by proposing an Embodied Interaction Framework, developed and examined in an online graduation show set within a virtual model of a campus building, to shift visitors from passive viewers to active agents of analysis and critique. The framework comprises three levels, with spatial controls enabling perspective shifts across scales, object-level tools supporting inspection and comparison, and a contextual layer coupling exhibits with spatiotemporal architectural settings to guide interpretation. Based on qualitative feedback following a session of free exploration, this framework enables analytical engagement by first anchoring users in a familiar spatial presence and then providing interaction that transcends physical constraints to deepen comprehension beyond static display. This study offers a transferable model applying to virtual exhibition design and the broader practice of embodied space design.

31st International Conference of the Association for Computer-Aided Architectural Design Research in Asia, CAADRIA 2026, Vol. 3, pp. 287–296, 26 April–2 May 2026.
